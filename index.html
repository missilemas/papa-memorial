His Service

Staff Sergeant Lawrence E. Simon flew as a waist gunner on B-17 Flying Fortresses with the 427th Bomb Squadron, 303rd Bomb Group, Eighth Air Force, at Molesworth, England. The 303rd was known throughout the war as “Hell’s Angels,” a name it took from one of its own B-17s.

He joined the 427th on 17 August 1944 with the crew of Lt (later Capt) George C. Newton. Group mission reports list him on 32 combat missions between 30 August 1944 and 3 February 1945; the crew photograph taken at Molesworth on 30 December 1944 records the completion of 35 missions. His missions included support for Operation Market Garden, three trips to the oil refineries at Merseburg, Christmas Eve 1944, St. Vith during the Battle of the Bulge, and Berlin.

Through 8 January 1945 he is listed as waist gunner. On his later missions the reports list him as toggler, the crewman who released the bombs on the lead ship’s signal (and once, on 29 January 1945, in the bombardier position). According to the 303rd Bomb Group’s crew history, every man of the Newton crew except the co-pilot completed his combat tour without injury, Lawrence among them.

The crew flew their first 13 missions together, #234 (30 Aug 1944) through #253 (7 Oct 1944). Newton then became a lead-crew pilot; the officers flew as fill-ins with other pilots, while the enlisted men flew most of their remaining missions with Newton.

“Queenie”

Queenie
GN-Q
B-17G · 42-97281

The Newton crew’s B-17G “Queenie,” serial 42-97281, squadron code GN-Q, flew 78 missions with the 427th.

16 November 1944, Mission #273 to Weisweiler: bad weather diverted the returning bombers to RAF Sleap. Queenie was the 30th to land. With her brakes failing, Lt Newton tried to ground-loop her; her right wing struck a parked B-17 and she came to rest hub-deep in mud. No one was injured. She was dug out and returned to service, and Lawrence flew in her again at St. Vith on 3 January 1945.

Queenie was later damaged on the 28 March 1945 Berlin mission and crash-landed near Sandwich, Kent, with another crew.

Remembering “Red” Raley

2nd Lieutenant Stonewall Jackson “Red” Raley flew as Lawrence’s co-pilot on 16 of his 23 missions. On 21 November 1944, flying with Lt Peter F. Cureton’s crew on the mission to the Leuna refinery at Merseburg, he was lost. He came safely to earth by parachute, and was killed on the ground.

Lawrence flew to Merseburg that same day with the Newton crew. Red Raley stands in the crew’s photograph as an inset, kept among the men he flew with.

2LT STONEWALL J. RALEY · 21 NOVEMBER 1944

A Note on “Hell’s Angels”

The famous B-17F “Hell’s Angels” (41-24577, VK-D) flew with the 358th Bomb Squadron of the 303rd Bomb Group. On 13 May 1943 it became one of the first Eighth Air Force bombers to complete 25 missions, and the whole 303rd took its name. Lawrence served in the same group, “Hell’s Angels,” but in its 427th Squadron.

The 327th Bomb Squadron, sometimes linked with this story, belonged to a different group, the 92nd Bomb Group (“Fame’s Favored Few”). No record of Lawrence E. Simon was found with that unit.
